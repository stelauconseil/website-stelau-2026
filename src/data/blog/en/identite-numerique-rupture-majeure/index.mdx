**The best customer database might be the one you never built.**

Since 2002, Nicolas Chalanset and I have championed one conviction at Stelau: authenticating people and checking their rights should not require collecting their digital lives.

The wallet opens that door. Information systems now need to step through it.

> Less data to keep. Less data to have stolen.

## Ten documents to teach a class. Seriously?

For a teaching assignment, I received this request. Identity, diplomas, insurance, criminal record, bank details… A complete file, soon scattered across email, applications and backups.

<DocumentCollection lang="en"/>

Every request has a purpose. But **checking a qualification does not require keeping a diploma copy everywhere**. We automated collection. It is time to automate verification.

## Tomorrow’s systems will know how to ask again

**STOP COLLECTING · VERIFY AS NEEDED · KEEP MINIMAL EVIDENCE.**

Request what matters. Verify it. Use it. Delete what no longer needs to be retained. Ask again when the need returns. A one-off use should no longer become a permanent record by default.

**Sending a letter?** Obtain the address for that delivery, then remove it from the business workflow. Or delegate dispatch to an authorised provider without copying the address into every application.

**Sending an email later?** Arrange a bounded contact channel: a relationship-specific alias, relay or notification. One purpose, an expiry, revocation. Without a channel or mandate arranged in advance, a new interaction with the person will be needed.

**The hotel invoice?** Ask for the necessary details at the right moment. Keep the invoice under applicable obligations. Remove unnecessary working copies.

These architectures still need to be built and integrated. An alias or relay reference may remain personal data; sharing approval does not replace a lawful basis for processing. **Storing less does not mean losing the ability to operate. It means designing differently.**

## Useful evidence, not your entire life

A purpose-built qualification credential makes the principle clear. The issuer signs a credential bound to your key. The RP requests the necessary attributes; you approve sharing; the wallet produces proof for this exchange.

<PresentationFlow lang="en"/>

The issuer’s signature protects the attested attributes. The holder’s proof binds the presentation to its recipient and the exchange’s challenge. **Another service cannot simply replay it as a valid response to its own request.** This does not stop the first recipient copying information it received: share less in the first place.

A retained record must support a specific decision, for a defined period. It is not automatically anonymous or re-auditable cryptographic evidence. Replacing a file of copies with a vault of complete presentations would miss the point.

## 2026–2031: stop collecting by default

Five moves for the next five years. A direction for work, not a regulatory timetable.

<IdentityRoadmap lang="en"/>

We do not need to wait for “ZKP everywhere”. Focused credentials and data obtained when needed already offer a starting point. Outages, lost phones and accessible alternatives must also be designed in: data minimisation has to work in real life.

## The wallet is only the beginning

Issuers and wallets will continue to retain data. The shift is to **stop copying it into every information system**.

At Stelau, our work on [wallets](/en/expertise/wallets-identite-numerique/), applied cryptography and [Nested VDS](/en/work/nested-vds/) pursues that target: **from data to evidence, from storage to verification**.
